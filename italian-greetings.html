Italian greetings: hello, goodbye and thank you

How to say hello, goodbye, please and thank you in Italian, with simple pronunciation. And the part most phrasebooks skip: when to say ciao, when buongiorno and when buonasera.

Saying hello: ciao, salve, buongiorno

Ciao! Hi! / Bye! (informal only) chow

Salve! Hello (polite, neutral) SAL-veh

Buongiorno! Good morning / Good day bwon-JOR-no

Buon pomeriggio! Good afternoon (less common) bwon po-meh-REE-jo

Buonasera! Good evening bwo-na-SEH-ra

Buonanotte! Good night bwo-na-NOT-teh

Good morning and good day are the same word in Italian: buongiorno. From the late afternoon Italians switch to buonasera. Buonanotte is not a greeting when you arrive: you say it when you go to bed, or when you leave late at night.

Not sure whether to be formal or informal? Say salve. It is polite and works almost everywhere: in a shop, at a hotel, with a neighbour.

Saying goodbye

Arrivederci! Goodbye ar-ree-veh-DER-chee

Ciao! Bye! (informal) chow

A presto! See you soon a PREH-sto

A dopo! See you later (today) a DOH-po

A domani! See you tomorrow a do-MA-nee

Ci vediamo! See you chee veh-DYA-mo

Buona giornata! Have a nice day BWO-na jor-NA-ta

Buona serata! Have a nice evening BWO-na seh-RA-ta

When someone wishes you Buona giornata!, answer Grazie, altrettanto! ("Thank you, you too!").

Thank you, please, sorry

Grazie Thank you GRA-tsyeh

Grazie mille Thank you very much GRA-tsyeh MEEL-leh

Prego You're welcome / Go ahead PREH-go

Per favore Please per fa-VO-reh

Scusa Sorry / Excuse me (informal) SKOO-za

Scusi Sorry / Excuse me (formal) SKOO-zee

Mi dispiace I'm sorry mee dee-SPYA-cheh

Permesso? Excuse me (when passing) per-MES-so

English speakers often mix up prego and per favore. When you ask for something, it is per favore: Un caffè, per favore. When you answer a thank you, or offer something, it is prego.

Introductions and "how are you?"

Piacere! Nice to meet you pya-CHEH-reh

Come ti chiami? What's your name? (informal) KO-meh tee KYA-mee

Come si chiama? What's your name? (formal) KO-meh see KYA-ma

Mi chiamo Anna. My name is Anna. mee KYA-mo

Come stai? How are you? (informal) KO-meh sty

Come sta? How are you? (formal) KO-meh sta

Bene, grazie. E tu? Fine, thanks. And you? BEH-neh GRA-tsyeh, eh too

Sono di Londra. I'm from London. SO-no dee LON-dra

Tu or Lei? Informal and formal

Italian has two ways to say "you" to one person. The formal one, Lei, uses the verb in the third person: that is why it is Come stai? with a friend and Come sta? with a stranger.

  • Tu (informal): family, friends, children, young people among themselves. This is where ciao and scusa belong.
  • Lei (formal): shops, restaurants, offices, older people, anyone you do not know. This is where buongiorno, buonasera and scusi belong.

When an Italian says Diamoci del tu!, they are inviting you to switch to the informal "you".

At the table and before a trip

Buon appetito! Enjoy your meal bwon ap-peh-TEE-to

Salute! / Cin cin! Cheers! sa-LOO-teh / chin chin

Auguri! Best wishes / Congratulations ow-GOO-ree

Buon viaggio! Safe travels / Have a good trip bwon VYAJ-jo

In bocca al lupo! Good luck! (answer: Crepi!) een BOK-ka al LOO-po

Three mistakes English speakers make most

  1. Ciao to strangers. In a shop, in a restaurant or with older people, say buongiorno, buonasera or salve.
  2. Buonanotte instead of buonasera. In the evening, when you arrive, it is always buonasera. Buonanotte is for bedtime.
  3. Prego instead of per favore. When you ask for something, say per favore. Prego is the answer to grazie.

Buongiorno! Prego? Good morning! What would you like? barista

Un caffè, per favore. A coffee, please. you

Ecco a Lei. Here you are. barista

Grazie mille. Buona giornata! Thank you very much. Have a nice day! you

Grazie, altrettanto! Thank you, you too! barista

Common questions

How do you say hello in Italian?

Ciao with friends and family, buongiorno (good morning / good day) or buonasera (good evening) with people you do not know. Salve is a polite hello that works with almost everyone.

How do you say good morning in Italian?

Buongiorno, pronounced bwon-JOR-no. Italians use it from the morning until the afternoon, then switch to buonasera.

What is the difference between ciao and salve?

Ciao is informal: use it with friends, family and people your age. Salve is neutral and polite, so it is the safe choice in a shop, a hotel or with a neighbour.

When do Italians switch from buongiorno to buonasera?

There is no exact rule. Usually around 4 or 5 pm, and in some regions straight after lunch. If you are not sure, say salve.

How do you say thank you and you're welcome in Italian?

Thank you is grazie, and thank you very much is grazie mille. The answer is prego, which means you're welcome.

How do you say goodbye in Italian?

Arrivederci is the polite goodbye. With friends you say ciao, a presto (see you soon) or a domani (see you tomorrow).

How do you say safe travels in Italian?

Buon viaggio! It means have a good trip. For good luck Italians say in bocca al lupo, and the answer is crepi.
