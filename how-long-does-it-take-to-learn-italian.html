How long does it take to learn Italian?

A realistic answer for English speakers: how many hours each level needs, what you can do at each stage, and a weekly plan that works.

By Lucia, native Italian teacher with a Master's in teaching Italian · Updated November 2026

The short answer

For English speakers, Italian is one of the faster major languages to learn. The US Foreign Service Institute places it among the languages easiest for English speakers, at roughly 600 to 750 class hours to reach professional working proficiency. That is the top of the scale, though. You can hold simple, useful conversations much sooner.

Language learning bodies use "guided learning hours" to estimate each level of the European framework (CEFR). These are approximate and vary by person, but they give a useful map:

LevelWhat you can doApproximate hours
A1Introduce yourself, order, ask simple questions60 to 100
A2Handle everyday situations, talk about your routine and past180 to 200
B1Follow most conversations, tell stories, give opinions350 to 400
B2Speak fluently on many topics, work in Italian500 to 600
C1Express yourself precisely, with nuance and idioms700 to 800

Hours are indicative estimates for the whole learning journey, including study outside lessons, and they are the same for every language. English speakers usually move faster in Italian, because so many words are familiar. Your own pace depends on your goals, your habits and how much you speak.

What this means in months

If you have one 60-minute lesson a week and study about 20 minutes on most days, you spend roughly 3 to 4 hours a week on Italian. In practice:

  • First 3 months: basic phrases, the present tense, simple conversations about yourself.
  • About 6 months: everyday situations, the past tenses, comfortable travel Italian (around A2).
  • About 1 year: real conversations, films with fewer subtitles, stories and opinions (around B1).
  • About 2 years: fluent on many topics, work emails and meetings (around B2).

Two lessons a week, or living in an Italian-speaking environment, can shorten this a lot.

What speeds you up

  1. Speak from day one. Understanding comes from using the language, not only from reading rules.
  2. Get corrected. A teacher stops mistakes becoming habits, especially with pronunciation and verb endings.
  3. Little and often. Fifteen minutes a day beats a three-hour session once a week.
  4. Learn what you need. Choose topics that match your life: work, travel, family.
  5. Review. Revisit new words after a day, a week and a month.

A simple weekly plan

  • One lesson a week, with a teacher: online or in Prague.
  • Five short sessions of 15 to 20 minutes: homework, vocabulary, one short podcast or video.
  • One "fun" hour: a film, a song or a recipe in Italian.

Common questions

How long does it take to become fluent in Italian?

Most people call B2 "fluent": you speak comfortably on many topics and can work in Italian. With one lesson a week and 20 minutes most days, English speakers usually get there in about two years. With two lessons a week and daily practice, it can take about a year.

How quickly can you learn Italian?

It depends on your hours, not your talent. An intensive course of 15 to 20 hours a week can take you to everyday Italian (A2) in about three months. With a regular weekly rhythm, simple conversations come after about three months and comfortable travel Italian after about six.

How long does it take to learn Italian if you live in Italy?

Living in Italy speeds you up only if you use Italian every day: at the shops, with colleagues, with friends. Combined with lessons, many people reach everyday Italian within a few months and real conversations within about a year. If your life stays in English, moving to Italy changes surprisingly little.

Can you learn Italian on your own, and how long does it take?

Apps and books are good for vocabulary and reading, and you can reach a basic level (A1 to A2) on your own in a few months. Speaking is the hard part: without conversation and correction, self-learners usually understand much more than they can say. A weekly lesson closes that gap.

How long does it take the average person to learn Italian?

With one lesson a week and a little practice most days, a typical adult learner holds simple conversations after about three months, manages comfortable travel Italian (A2) in about six months, reaches B1 in about a year and B2 in about two.
