Essential Italian phrases for travel

The phrases you will actually use in Italy, with simple pronunciation, and five rules that make your Italian easier to understand.

Greetings and politeness

Ciao! Hi! / Bye! (informal) chow

Buongiorno Good morning / Good day bwon-JOR-no

Buonasera Good evening bwo-na-SEH-ra

Arrivederci Goodbye ar-ree-veh-DER-chee

Per favore Please per fa-VO-re

Grazie Thank you GRA-tsyeh

Prego You're welcome / Please, go ahead PREH-go

Scusi Excuse me (formal) SKOO-zee

Cafés and restaurants

Un caffè, per favore. A coffee, please. oon kaf-FEH

Un tavolo per due. A table for two. oon TA-vo-lo per DOO-eh

Il menù, per favore. The menu, please. eel me-NOO

Vorrei una pizza margherita. I would like a margherita pizza. vor-RAY OO-na PEE-tsa mar-ge-REE-ta

Il conto, per favore. The bill, please. eel KON-to

Sono vegetariano/a. I am vegetarian. SO-no ve-je-ta-RYA-no

Getting around

Dov'è la stazione? Where is the station? do-VEH la sta-TSYO-ne

Un biglietto per Roma. A ticket to Rome. oon beel-LYET-to per RO-ma

A che ora parte il treno? What time does the train leave? a keh O-ra PAR-te eel TREH-no

Quanto costa? How much does it cost? KWAN-to KO-sta

A destra / a sinistra / dritto Right / left / straight on a DEH-stra / a see-NEE-stra / DREET-to

When you need help

Non capisco. I do not understand. non ka-PEE-sko

Può parlare più lentamente? Can you speak more slowly? pwo par-LA-re pyoo len-ta-MEN-te

Parla inglese? Do you speak English? PAR-la een-GLEH-ze

Mi può aiutare? Can you help me? mee pwo a-yoo-TA-re

Five pronunciation rules that help immediately

  1. Say every vowel clearly. Italian vowels are short and pure: a, e, i, o, u, always the same sound.
  2. C and G are soft before e and i (ciao "chow", gelato "je-LA-to"), and hard before a, o, u and h (caffè, spaghetti "spa-GET-tee").
  3. CH and GH are always hard: che "keh", ghiaccio "GYAH-cho".
  4. Double consonants are longer: pala (shovel) and palla (ball) are different words.
  5. GN sounds like "ny", GLI like "ly": gnocchi "NYOK-kee", famiglia "fa-MEE-lya".
